1. AI Market Trends

OpenAI used DevDay to move from disclosure to product, while press coverage says it conceded the shelved model and pushed back its IPO. ✅ OpenAI's DevDay recap and its GPT-6.1 Sol post (both read at source, September 29) describe GPT-6.1 Sol as available to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex and through the API as gpt-6.1-sol at $2 input, $0.10 cached input, and $10 output per million tokens — "one-fifth of Astra's standard API input and output token prices"; the post reports self-reported results including a 2.2-point lead over Claude Opus 5.5 on AutomationBench and a factuality error rate cut from 11.4% to 7.7% at low reasoning effort. ✅ The recap also lists "dots," always-on agents available to Pro and Business Premium plans in eligible markets; a new Pro 500 plan; Codex in the cloud; an Agents API with computer use; a Decisions API in limited preview; MCP Events for trigger-based plugin automation; and Bedrock Managed Agents powered by OpenAI on AWS (OpenAI, DevDay recap, read at source; the recap page was read through automated page summarization). 🔶 Press coverage (Axios, Yahoo Tech) reports that OpenAI acknowledged shelving GPT-6.1 Astra, that future Astra models remain in development, and that Altman said OpenAI will not pursue an IPO in 2026; Gizmodo quotes him: "We have got to be able to make confident safety claims." ⚠️ The CNBC live blog returned an access error, and we found no OpenAI post stating the IPO position or the cancellation itself, so both remain press-sourced. 🔶 The Hacker News, citing Reuters, quotes OpenAI head of safety systems Saachi Jain saying the model "didn't quite meet the bar in terms of staying within scope and authorization."

Six AI leaders signed a voluntary safety accord at the White House. ✅ Al Jazeera reports that on September 29 the leaders of Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, xAI, and Nvidia signed a "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities," pledging "robust" safeguards including internal monitoring teams, third-party audits, and regular meetings to set safety standards; the accord is non-binding and says some measures "may make sense" to codify into law eventually (Al Jazeera, read at source). ✅ Al Jazeera also quotes critics, including Toby Walsh, who question why an industry "marks its own homework." ⚠️ One aggregator summary we read also described a separate executive order renaming "artificial intelligence"; we found no corroboration and are not carrying it.

Anthropic: leaked prospectus coverage continues; no public filing is confirmed. ✅ Fortune reports the prospectus was "leaked to Reuters" and describes it as a draft; Anthropic has not commented in the article we read (Fortune, read at source). ✅ Figures attributed to the draft by Fortune: 2025 revenue of $4.6 billion, an operating loss of about $8.06 billion, a net loss of $42 billion, year-end 2025 cash of $20.28 billion, $518 billion in cloud and data-center commitments, and two customers accounting for nearly a quarter of 2025 revenue (Fortune, read at source; unconfirmed by any SEC filing we could read). ⚠️ Timing reports conflict: Reuters-based coverage says a listing could come after the November U.S. midterm elections, while earlier Fortune and Bloomberg-sourced (via Quartz) reports pointed to an October Nasdaq listing at a valuation near $2 trillion.
