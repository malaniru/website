Executive Summary

The Security Council meeting produced one clear point of agreement: the two largest labs both asked governments for incident reporting and common testing standards. ✅ Sam Altman told the Council the world needs "accurate and speedy incident reporting," common standards for measuring capabilities and judging safeguards, and secure channels between governments, infrastructure operators and technical experts; he also said OpenAI has "unilaterally slowed down in the past" and "will do so in the future" (OpenAI, remarks as delivered, read at source). 🔶 Dario Amodei, appearing by video, proposed narrow global agreements such as a ban on using AI to build biological weapons, verification systems, and common testing standards with a notification system for AI security incidents (CNN and others, search level). ✅ Anthropic reported that Claude agents found a previously uncharacterized enzyme system in bacteriophage DNA after 21 hours of searching, with all lab work done by human scientists (primary, read at source). ✅ Markets moved on bond yields, not AI news: the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.13% to 26,936.04 as the 10-year Treasury yield reached its highest level since 2007 (read at source).

1. AI Market Trends

Governance talk has moved from the labs to the Security Council, and the two lab chiefs asked for much the same thing. ✅ France convened the meeting, with Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot chairing; the briefers were Yoshua Bengio, Sam Altman, Dario Amodei and Clément Delangue (Security Council Report, read at source yesterday; UN News, read at source today). 🔶 DeepSeek and Moonshot were invited to make statements (search level, not confirmed at source). ✅ Altman's remarks named two failure modes: losing control of the future to AI, and concentrating "too much power in too few hands." He said standards "should not lock in incumbents" and that each government should decide how to adopt them (primary, read at source). 🔶 Amodei is reported as saying Anthropic "will slow down as much as necessary" to make sure each release is safe (France 24 headline and secondary coverage; ⚠️ transcript not read at source). 🔶 CNBC's headline says the appeal came after President Trump rejected what he called a "globalist scheme" to control AI (headline only; article not reachable).

Third-party assessment is now a published proposal, not just a talking point. ✅ On September 22 OpenAI published "Priorities and principles for effective third party assessments," naming four areas for outside review: safety cases, critical safeguards, capability evaluations tied to its Preparedness Framework, and investigation of serious misalignment incidents. Its principles include pre-agreed scope, proportionate access, disclosed conflicts of interest and responsible publication (primary, read at source). ⚠️ The framework is written by the company being assessed and creates no outside obligation.

The capital picture is dominated by macro pressure. ✅ The Institute of International Finance estimates $2.6 trillion of global spending on AI over the 12 months to mid-2026, against $3.5 trillion of interest paid by advanced economies on internationally traded bonds (IIF via Yahoo Finance, read at source). ✅ Goldman Sachs expects Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Microsoft to spend about $800 billion on capital expenditure this year (via Yahoo Finance, read at source). ✅ Crunchbase counts at least 114 Series A rounds of $100 million or more raised by startups worldwide so far this year (Crunchbase News, September 23, teaser read at source). ✅ Temporal Technologies, which builds a platform for long-running AI agents, raised $550 million at a $12.55 billion valuation (Crunchbase, week of September 12–18, read at source). 🔶 Snorkel AI is reported raising $350 million at $3.5 billion, and Ema $77 million (search level).
