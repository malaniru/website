Executive Summary

Anthropic launched the roadshow for its proposed IPO on Tuesday, November 10, offering 825,000,000 Class A shares at an expected $70.00 to $78.00 per share on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol "ANT." ✅ (Anthropic, roadshow announcement, November 10; company-reported; the registration statement is not yet effective). TechCrunch and Fortune, reading the amended filing, put the fully diluted valuation at up to $1.404 trillion at the top of the range and the October revenue run rate at about $81 billion; TechCrunch, citing CNBC, says trading could start close to November 19 🔶 (TechCrunch and Fortune, November 10; we did not read the filing). Nvidia fell for a second straight session on November 10, by about 5.5%, after its board opened an independent investigation into reports that its CEO was the target of a foreign intelligence effort ✅ (Nvidia Board statement, November 8; The Motley Fool, November 10). On the product side, IBM and Microsoft announced an enterprise agent platform and Google Cloud put an agent for hospitals and insurers into US preview ✅ (Microsoft Source, November 10; The Next Web, November 10). ⚠️ Anthropic's offering terms can still change before pricing, and no final price exists yet.

1. AI Market Trends

Anthropic's offering terms are now public. ✅ Anthropic's November 10 announcement says 825,000,000 shares are offered, 801,351,354 by Anthropic and 23,648,646 by existing stockholders; Anthropic will not receive proceeds from the selling stockholders; it expects to grant the underwriters a 30-day option on up to 123,750,000 more shares; and the price is expected to be between $70.00 and $78.00. Goldman Sachs & Co. LLC and Morgan Stanley & Co. LLC are listed as prospectus contacts (Anthropic, November 10; company-reported). ✅ At those terms the gross offering is $57.75 billion to $64.35 billion (TechCrunch, November 10; arithmetic matches 825 million shares times the range). ✅ Renaissance Capital's headline describes a "$61 billion IPO, targeting a $1.2 trillion market cap," with about 3% of shares secondary (Renaissance Capital, November 10; headline and summary only). ⚠️ The $1.2 trillion and $1.404 trillion figures use different bases (market cap versus fully diluted at the top of the range); do not mix them. This also supersedes the $75 billion raise estimate carried in earlier editions.

What the amended filing reportedly shows. 🔶 TechCrunch reports an October run-rate revenue of $81 billion, about $10 billion more than at the end of September; third-quarter revenue of $17 billion; nine-month revenue of $33.2 billion; a nine-month net loss of $104 billion; compute and infrastructure commitments of $900.9 billion; and nearly $132 billion in cash and short-term investments (TechCrunch, November 10; secondary reading of the filing). 🔶 TechCrunch and Fortune report a $26 billion private placement of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price to seven companies including Google and Nvidia, with Google and Nvidia investing $10 billion each (TechCrunch; Fortune, November 10). 🔶 The Motley Fool reports AMD plans to invest $2.5 billion in that placement, counting toward an earlier commitment of up to $5 billion tied to chip deliveries (The Motley Fool, November 10). ⚠️ We did not read the filing on EDGAR; check each figure there before repeating it. This answers the open question from our last edition: the roadshow did start this week, despite the investor doubts Reuters reported on November 6.

Safety disclosure in the filing. 🔶 Fortune reports the filing says internal estimates indicate Anthropic "may imminently meet" a Responsible Scaling Policy threshold for dramatically accelerated AI progress, and that no current model has crossed it (Fortune, November 10; quoting the filing).

Market backdrop. ✅ On November 10 the S&P 500 closed at 7,520.93, down 0.38%, and the Nasdaq Composite at about 25,066, down 0.74%, after October consumer prices rose 0.4% against an expected 0.3% (TheStreet, November 10). ✅ Nvidia's board said on November 8 that a committee of independent directors, with outside counsel, will investigate reporting that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, and that it has "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised (Nvidia, Statement from NVIDIA Board of Directors, November 8; company-reported).
