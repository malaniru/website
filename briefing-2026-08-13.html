Divergence Analysis

The three lenses agree on the deployable core and split on what is moving it. Relevance stays topped by MCP itself — the stateless spec is standing, extensions are live, downloads are past 400 million a month, and older clients will not interoperate. Momentum this session is an earnings story: the AI-infrastructure complex (CoreWeave, Super Micro, Nebius, Lumentum) posted blowout results that read the capex cycle as intact, Nvidia added 3% into its August 26 print, and Anthropic's firming IPO roadshow stays the freshest private-market thread. Maturity is unchanged: Opus 5 leads, GPT-5.6 is the mature competitor near 59, Kimi K3 is an assessable open-weight base, and Gemini 3.5 Pro is not — no model card 86 days after its I/O announcement. "Announced" and "assessable" remain different states; Opus 5 crossed that line July 24, Gemini 3.5 Pro still has not.

What This Means for You. A cooler CPI and an AI-earnings rally change nothing about the deployable stack: Opus 5 as the load-bearing default with an effort setting, GPT-5.6 as the mature competitor, Kimi K3 as a benchmarked open-weight base, with DeepSeek V4-Flash-0731 worth a price-performance look for cost-sensitive, non-sensitive workloads. The MCP migration remains the concrete engineering task — exercise every connector against the new Tier 1 SDKs, confirm nothing depends on the removed handshake or session header, and flag Enterprise-Managed Authorization for sensitive-data work. The infrastructure-earnings surge is a demand signal, not a delivery input: capacity is expanding, but model selection and governance still decide fit. Anthropic's IPO roadshow and next week's Nvidia print are tracking items, not changes to delivery.
