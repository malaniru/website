1. AI Market Trends

Intel's round trip is the week's tidiest illustration of the demand-versus-tape gap. The fundamentals did exactly what the bull case needed — a 59% jump in Data Center and AI, an 18A yield step from 65% to 85%, and a raised Q3 guide — and the stock still handed the entire after-hours pop back within a session, closing down about 7.89% at $92.32 on foundry-customer and AI-roadmap questions (Source: The Motley Fool, July 24; Yahoo Finance, July 24 — press). ✅ It fits the early-July pattern precisely: a semiconductor selloff that erased more than a trillion dollars of market value on valuation nerves and macro rather than any demand miss — "no disaster to explain it," as one account put it at the time (Source: ABC News, "What to know about the AI chip stock selloff," July 2026; Forbes, "Semiconductor Selloff Deepens As AI Spending Fears Hit Intel," July 8 — press). The read that survives the noise is unchanged: chip demand keeps confirming while the equities trade on multiples, roadmap risk, and rates.

Alphabet's record 2026 capex guide is still the quarter's largest single demand signal for the buildout. The guide — reported at $205 billion, with some coverage framing a $195–205 billion range — followed Q2 revenue up 24% to $119.8 billion and Google Cloud up about 82% to $24.8 billion, with second-quarter capex alone near $44.9 billion (Source: CNBC, "Alphabet earnings takeaways," July 22; Yahoo Finance / Reuters market wrap, July 23 — press). ⚠️ The precise headline figure still differs across outlets — a single $205 billion number versus a $195–205 billion range; treat the record framing as solid and the point figure as reported, not settled. Alphabet steadied Friday after its ~7% Thursday drop, but the market's read has not moved: the spend is demand confirmation for the chip complex and a free-cash-flow question for Alphabet's own shareholders.

The enterprise-agent adoption picture sharpened this week, and it is still a governance-gap story. Fresh survey data put harder numbers on the split that circulated all month. A Boomi/Forrester survey of 409 director-and-above technology decision-makers found 86% of organizations have moved past the agent-pilot stage while only 34% say they trust the actions their agents take (Source: Yahoo Finance, "86% Of Enterprises Have Deployed AI Agents. Just 34% Trust Them, Boomi Study Finds," July 2026 — press). Alongside it, Anaconda/Forrester data has 88% of agent pilots failing to reach production, with evaluation gaps (64%), governance friction (57%), and model reliability (51%) the top blockers, and Gartner's read is close at 89% of pilots failing while the surviving ~11% return a reported 171% ROI (Source: Forrester/Anaconda via Institute PM; Gartner via industry reporting, July 2026 — press/aggregator). ⚠️ These are survey figures — trace them to the primary instrument before any external use. The through-line is consistent and now better-sourced: deployment is widespread, trusted production is not, and the bottleneck is operational governance more than model capability.

Private capital keeps clearing above the compute layer. July agent-startup funding is tracked at roughly $1.8 billion across 12+ deals, valuations up about 40% quarter-over-quarter, most rounds Series B or later (Source: aifunding.me and Gravity funding trackers, July 2026 — aggregator; ⚠️ not traced to primary announcements; directional). The standout remains Moonshot AI, which has circulated a shareholder resolution for a Hong Kong listing within six months and is near a round valuing it above $30 billion on the strength of Kimi K3 — whose open weights publish Monday (Source: Yahoo Finance, "Moonshot AI Plans Hong Kong IPO," July 20; TrendingTopics, July 20 — press; ⚠️ valuation and timing reported, not company-confirmed). Money keeps arriving at the vertical-agent and open-weight layer even as the public market reprices the chips beneath it.

3. Agentic AI Advances & New Releases

  • MCP 2026-07-28 — three days out. Stateless core (removes the initialize handshake and the Mcp-Session-Id header), Extensions framework, Tasks and MCP Apps promoted to first-class, OAuth-aligned authorization hardening, and a formal deprecation policy. Any server that needed sticky sessions and a shared session store can now run behind a plain round-robin load balancer; RC locked since May 21, beta SDKs shipped (Source: modelcontextprotocol.io blog; WorkOS; SecurityWeek — primary/press).

Migrated calls use deepseek-v4-flash or deepseek-v4-pro; same base URL, key and request shape, but v4-flash defaults to thinking on, so pin thinking: disabled to reproduce the old non-thinking behavior (Source: DeepSeek API docs; Enterprise DNA; TECHi, July 2026 — primary/press).
  • Kimi K3 — weights Monday, July 27. 2.8T-parameter MoE, ~50B active per token, native multimodal, 1M context; MXFP4 weights with a ~1.4TB footprint (roughly 600GB in INT4), so self-hosting needs serious accelerator capacity. Announced July 16; weights and technical report by July 27 (Source: TECHi, "Kimi K3's open weights arrive July 27. The catch is 1.4TB," July 2026; cryptobriefing; Interconnects — press/vendor-adjacent).
  • Claude — Add-ins and Copilot reach. A new Add-ins capability lets Claude operate directly inside productivity software (e.g., Microsoft Word) rather than through a connector, Microsoft 365 Copilot has added Claude and Cowork agent workflows, and Anthropic has reserved the "Claude Browser" MCP server name ahead of a Desktop pane rename (Source: Windows Forum; Fingerlakes1; Releasebot, July 2026 — press/secondary).
  • Google shipped Flash, not Pro. The July 21 batch — Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and a Flash "Cyber" variant — landed, but Gemini 3.5 Pro is still not out; Google's position is "testing with partners," no date (Source: TechCrunch, July 21; CroeAi, July 2026 — press).

What This Means for You. Two items here have hard dates and touch live deployments. The DeepSeek alias retirement is behind us — a good moment to confirm no integration is still calling the dead names, since the default rename switches reasoning on and changes token cost and latency. MCP is next: three days to final, and any connector built against the initialize handshake or protocol-level session IDs should be exercised against the beta SDKs this weekend — the deprecation runway covers older primitives, not the stateless-core changes landing on the 28th. The new Cowork Add-ins surface is worth a note of its own: working "inside" an app rather than through a connector changes the data-handling story, and it is worth understanding before adopting it for sensitive documents.
