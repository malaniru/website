Rates, not equities, were the event. The 10-year Treasury yield rose as high as 5.04% on Tuesday, described as the highest since 2007, and closed at 5.00% (press, multi-source). 🔶 Coverage attributes the move to a global bond selloff driven by capital-investment demand and energy prices, with oil rising again on concern that a widening Middle East conflict could further constrain crude supply (press). 🔶

That "highest since 2007" framing appears to contradict this series and does not. ✅ On Monday this briefing carried a 5.012% intraday high described as the highest since October 2023. Tuesday's 5.04% is described as the highest since 2007. Both can be true, and both are: the October 2023 peak sits between the two figures, so Monday's high approached it and Tuesday's high cleared it. The distinction is worth stating because a reader tracking this series day over day would otherwise be right to suspect one of the two outlets of an error, and neither made one. ⚠️ This briefing did not verify the October 2023 peak level at a primary source this run, so the reconciliation is offered as the most consistent reading of two press descriptions rather than as a confirmed fact.

The industry's public argument about pace hardened into two camps in a single day. At the Dreamforce main keynote in San Francisco, Marc Benioff appeared with both Dario Amodei and Jensen Huang (press, multi-source). 🔶 Amodei proposed a three-step approach to tempering the rate at which model capabilities improve without, in his framing, sacrificing commercial advantage or the United States' lead, and stressed the need for globally agreed standards; reporting states that Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis voiced support (press, multi-source). 🔶 Huang argued market forces already supply the discipline, that the industry needs no new laws or regulations, and that speed and safety are "not mutually exclusive" — he called the trade-off a "false choice" — while allowing that companies should pace themselves until confident in what they release (press, multi-source). 🔶 Mark Zuckerberg sided with Huang, arguing that competition, liability and user trust already give every lab the responsibility, ability and incentive to slow its own work when safety requires it, and that "trust and alignment" are themselves capabilities (press, multi-source). 🔶 ⚠️ Amodei also offered a growth argument on the same stage — that even with capability frozen, the economy is using "maybe 5-10%" of the available value, with "so much diffusion left" (press, single-source). 🔶 That is a striking thing to say in the same appearance as a slowdown proposal, and the two are only compatible if diffusion and capability are treated as separable. Whether they are is the actual question underneath this week's argument, and no participant addressed it directly in the coverage located this run.

What This Means for You. What This Means for You.
