1. AI Market Trends

Intel's round trip is still the tape's tidiest illustration of the demand-versus-price gap, and two days of closed markets have not resolved it. The fundamentals did what the bull case needed — Q2 revenue of $16.1 billion (+25% year-over-year), adjusted EPS of $0.42 against a ~$0.21 consensus, Data Center and AI up about 59%, an 18A yield step from 65% to 85%, and a raised Q3 guide to a $16.3 billion midpoint — and the stock still handed back its ~13% after-hours pop within a single session to close down about 7.89% at $92.32 (Source: The Motley Fool, July 24; Yahoo Finance, July 24 — press). ✅ It fits the early-July pattern exactly: a semiconductor selloff that erased more than a trillion dollars of market value on valuation nerves and macro, not any demand miss (Source: ABC News, "What to know about the AI chip stock selloff," July 2026; Forbes, "Semiconductor Selloff Deepens As AI Spending Fears Hit Intel," July 8 — press). The read that survives the weekend is unchanged: chip demand keeps confirming while the equities trade on multiples, roadmap risk, and rates.

Alphabet's record 2026 capex guide remains the quarter's largest single demand signal for the buildout. The guide — reported at $205 billion, with some coverage framing a $195–205 billion range — followed Q2 revenue up 24% to $119.8 billion and Google Cloud up about 82% to $24.8 billion, with second-quarter capex alone near $44.9 billion (Source: CNBC, "Alphabet earnings takeaways," July 22; Yahoo Finance / Reuters market wrap, July 23 — press). ⚠️ The precise headline figure still differs across outlets — a single $205 billion number versus a $195–205 billion range; treat the record framing as solid and the point figure as reported, not settled. The market's read has not moved: the spend is demand confirmation for the chip complex and a free-cash-flow question for Alphabet's own shareholders.

The enterprise-agent adoption picture is still a governance-gap story, and the survey figures that firmed up last week are the clearest numbers on it. A Boomi/Forrester survey of 409 director-and-above technology decision-makers found 86% of organizations have moved past the agent-pilot stage while only 34% say they trust the actions their agents take (Source: Yahoo Finance, "86% Of Enterprises Have Deployed AI Agents. Just 34% Trust Them, Boomi Study Finds," July 2026 — press). Alongside it, Anaconda/Forrester data has 88% of agent pilots failing to reach production, with evaluation gaps (64%), governance friction (57%), and model reliability (51%) the top blockers, and Gartner's read is close at 89% of pilots failing while the surviving ~11% return a reported 171% ROI (Source: Institute PM citing Forrester/Anaconda; Gartner via industry reporting, July 2026 — press/aggregator). ⚠️ These are survey figures — trace them to the primary instrument before any external use. The through-line is consistent: deployment is widespread, trusted production is not, and the bottleneck is operational governance more than model capability.

Private capital keeps clearing above the compute layer. July agent-startup funding is tracked at roughly $1.8 billion across 12+ deals, valuations up about 40% quarter-over-quarter, most rounds Series B or later (Source: aifunding.me and Gravity funding trackers, July 2026 — aggregator; ⚠️ not traced to primary announcements; directional). The standout remains Moonshot AI, which has circulated a shareholder resolution for a Hong Kong listing within six months and is near a round valuing it above $30 billion on the strength of Kimi K3 — whose open weights publish tomorrow (Source: Yahoo Finance, "Moonshot AI Plans Hong Kong IPO," July 20; TrendingTopics, July 20 — press; ⚠️ valuation and timing reported, not company-confirmed). Money keeps arriving at the vertical-agent and open-weight layer even as the public market reprices the chips beneath it.

3. Agentic AI Advances & New Releases

  • MCP 2026-07-28 — two days out. Stateless core (removes the initialize handshake and the Mcp-Session-Id header), Extensions framework, Tasks and MCP Apps promoted to first-class, OAuth-aligned authorization hardening, and a formal deprecation policy. Any server that needed sticky sessions and a shared session store can now run behind a plain round-robin load balancer; the RC has been locked since May 21 and beta Python, TypeScript, Go and C# SDKs shipped (Source: modelcontextprotocol.io blog; WorkOS; The Register, "Model Context Protocol prepares to break with its stateful past," July 23 — primary/press).
  • Kimi K3 — open weights tomorrow, Monday, July 27. 2.8T-parameter MoE, ~50B active per token (only 16 of 896 experts fire), native multimodal, 1M context; MXFP4 weights with a ~1.4TB footprint (versus ~5.6TB at 16-bit), so self-hosting needs serious accelerator capacity. It introduces "Kimi Delta Attention," claimed to decode up to ~6.3× faster than standard approaches, and the weights publish under a Modified MIT license. Announced July 16; weights and technical report by July 27 (Source: TechTimes, "Kimi K3 Open Weights Drop July 27," July 24; TECHi; Hugging Face model overview; Interconnects — press/vendor-adjacent). ⚠️ The speed and quality claims are vendor-stated until independent evaluation.
  • DeepSeek legacy aliases retired Friday. deepseek-chat and deepseek-reasoner stopped resolving at 15:59 UTC on July 24 — a hard cutoff, no fallback. Migrated calls use deepseek-v4-flash or deepseek-v4-pro; same base URL, key and request shape, but v4-flash defaults to thinking on, so pin thinking: disabled to reproduce the old non-thinking behavior (Source: DeepSeek API docs; Enterprise DNA; TECHi, July 2026 — primary/press).
  • Claude — Add-ins and Copilot reach. A new Add-ins capability lets Claude operate directly inside productivity software (e.g., Microsoft Word) rather than through a connector, and Microsoft 365 Copilot has added Claude and Cowork agent workflows (Source: Windows Forum; Fingerlakes1; Releasebot, July 2026 — press/secondary).
  • Google shipped Flash, not Pro. The July 21 batch — Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and a Flash "Cyber" variant — landed, but Gemini 3.5 Pro is still not out; Google's position is "testing with partners," no date (Source: TechCrunch, July 21; CroeAi, July 2026 — press).

What This Means for You. Two of these have hard dates and touch live deployments. The DeepSeek alias retirement is behind us — a good moment to confirm no integration is still calling the dead names, since the default rename switches reasoning on and changes token cost and latency. MCP is next: two days to final, and any connector built against the initialize handshake or protocol-level session IDs should be exercised against the beta SDKs before Tuesday — the deprecation runway covers older primitives, not the stateless-core changes landing on the 28th. The Cowork Add-ins surface is worth a note of its own: working "inside" an app rather than through a connector changes the data-handling story, and it is worth understanding before adopting it for sensitive documents.
